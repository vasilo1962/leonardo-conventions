Multi Landy

Application, examples and responses

When does it apply?

Multi Landy is a conventional defence to an opponent's natural 1NTone no trump opening.

Before using it, agree with partner whether you play it against a strong 1NTone no trump, a weak 1NTone no trump, or both.

In the version presented here, double is for penalties and 2♦two diamonds shows one long suit, which can be any of the four suits.

What is its purpose?

It lets us describe hands with one long suit or two suits. This helps partner choose a suitable trump suit and makes it harder for the opponents to find their own contract.

Most of these overcalls are conventional: they do not necessarily show the suit we bid.

The decision to overcall depends on distribution, suit quality, vulnerability and the strength of the opponents' 1NTone no trump.

A penalty double is not based solely on counting points. We need defensive tricks and a suitable opening lead.

Examples of overcalls

In the following examples, our right-hand opponent opens 1NTone no trump. For the distributional overcalls, assume we are not vulnerable.

1. Both majors

♠ KQ96spades: king, queen, 9, 6.
♥ AJ853hearts: ace, jack, 8, 5, 3.
♦ 7diamonds: 7.
♣ Q62clubs: queen, 6, 2.

We bid 2♣two clubs. We have 5 hearts and 4 spades. We describe both majors with one bid and let partner choose.

2. One long suit

♠ AQJ964spades: ace, queen, jack, 9, 6, 4.
♥ 852hearts: 8, 5, 2.
♦ K72diamonds: king, 7, 2.
♣ 7clubs: 7.

We bid 2♦two diamonds, showing a one-suited hand. We do not bid 2♠two spades, because in Multi Landy that overcall also promises a minor.

3. Hearts and a minor

♠ 7spades: 7.
♥ KQJ86hearts: king, queen, jack, 8, 6.
♦ AQ974diamonds: ace, queen, 9, 7, 4.
♣ 82clubs: 8, 2.

We bid 2♥two hearts. We show 5 hearts and a minor. Partner knows about the hearts but does not yet know which minor we hold.

4. Spades and a minor

♠ KJ853spades: king, jack, 8, 5, 3.
♥ 6hearts: 6.
♦ 84diamonds: 8, 4.
♣ AQ974clubs: ace, queen, 9, 7, 4.

We bid 2♠two spades. We show 5 spades and a minor, clubs in this case.

5. Both minors

♠ 7spades: 7.
♥ 84hearts: 8, 4.
♦ KQJ96diamonds: king, queen, jack, 9, 6.
♣ AJ874clubs: ace, jack, 8, 7, 4.

We bid 2NTtwo no trump. We show at least 5 clubs and 5 diamonds. This 2NTtwo no trump is conventional and does not show a balanced hand.

6. A penalty double

♠ AQJspades: ace, queen, jack.
♥ KQ4hearts: king, queen, 4.
♦ KJ93diamonds: king, jack, 9, 3.
♣ K82clubs: king, 8, 2.

Against a weak 1NTone no trump, for example 12–14 points, we double. We have a strong hand with defensive values. We want to defend 1NTone no trump doubled, not ask partner to choose a suit.

How do we respond?

The following responses assume that the next opponent passes.

After the 2♣two clubs overcall

We try to find the best major-suit fit.

ResponseResponse MeaningMeaning
2♥ / 2♠two hearts / two spades Preference for that major, without inviting game.Preference for that major, without inviting game.
2♦two diamonds Equal length in the majors. Ask partner to bid the longer major.Equal length in the majors. Ask partner to bid the longer major.
3♥ / 3♠three hearts / three spades At least 4-card support and a game invitation.At least 4-card support and a game invitation.

An example of preference

♠ A842spades: ace, 8, 4, 2.
♥ 97hearts: 9, 7.
♦ J843diamonds: jack, 8, 4, 3.
♣ 975clubs: 9, 7, 5.

We respond 2♠two spades. We have 4 spades and only 2 hearts. Partner has at least 4 spades, so we have at least 8 trumps together.

With the hand from the first overcall example, partner passes. Partner does not correct to the 5-card heart suit, since we have shown a preference for spades.

An example with no preference

♠ 852spades: 8, 5, 2.
♥ Q96hearts: queen, 9, 6.
♦ A843diamonds: ace, 8, 4, 3.
♣ 974clubs: 9, 7, 4.

We respond 2♦two diamonds. We have 3 cards in each major and leave the choice to partner.

With the hand from the first example, partner bids 2♥two hearts. We find an 8-card heart fit rather than a 7-card spade fit.

After the 2♦two diamonds overcall

We do not yet know partner's long suit. Our usual response is 2♥two hearts, which is pass or correct: partner passes with hearts, otherwise bids the long suit.

The overcaller:

  • Passes with hearts.
  • Bids 2♠two spades with spades.
  • Bids 3♣three clubs with clubs.
  • Bids 3♦three diamonds with diamonds.

Example

With the second overcall hand, the auction develops as follows:

WestWest NorthNorth EastEast SouthSouth
1NTone no trump 2♦two diamonds PassPass 2♥two hearts
PassPass 2♠two spades

South's 2♥two hearts did not show hearts. It allowed North to reveal the long suit.

A 2♠two spades response is also available when we are willing to play at least 3♥three hearts if partner has hearts. It is not a natural bid showing spades. Strong responses, such as the 2NTtwo no trump enquiry, require additional agreements about the continuations.

After a 2♥two hearts or 2♠two spades overcall

We know partner's major but not the minor.

Example

Partner overcalls 2♥two hearts with the third overcall hand. We do not want to play in hearts, so we ask for the minor with 2NTtwo no trump. Partner bids 3♦three diamonds.

Here, 2NTtwo no trump does not suggest playing in notrump.

After the 2NTtwo no trump overcall

We know partner has at least 5–5 in the minors.

With a weak hand, choose 3♣three clubs or 3♦three diamonds according to our support. We do not pass the conventional 2NTtwo no trump simply because we have few points.

With a strong hand, consider game, taking account of the fit, tricks and stoppers.

After the penalty double

We usually pass and defend 1NTone no trump doubled.

With a very weak hand and a suit of our own with at least 5 cards, we may make a natural escape bid. We do not automatically run to a 4-card suit merely because we have very few points.

Points to remember

  • 2♣two clubs shows the majors.
  • 2♦two diamonds shows one long suit.
  • 2♥two hearts and 2♠two spades overcalls also promise a minor.
  • 2NTtwo no trump as an overcall shows the minors, but as a response to 2♥two hearts or 2♠two spades asks for the minor.
  • Versions of Multi Landy differ. In particular, the meaning of double, whether 2♦two diamonds includes a minor, and invitational or preemptive raises must be agreed by the partnership.
