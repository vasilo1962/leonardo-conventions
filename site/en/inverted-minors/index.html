Inverted Minors

Application, examples and responses

When does it apply?

Inverted Minors applies after partner's natural 1♣one club or 1♦one diamond opening, when the opponents have neither overcalled nor doubled.

Here we use the version in which a single raise shows 10+ high-card points and is forcing for one round, while a jump raise shows 6–9 high-card points and may be passed. This is distinct from Two Way Inverted Minors.

Responder has no four-card major. With four hearts or four spades, respond in the major first so that a possible fit there is not lost.

What is its purpose?

“Inverted” reverses the usual strength relationship between the single raise and the jump raise: raising to the two level is stronger than jumping to the three level.

With invitational or stronger values, keep the bidding low. This leaves room to describe our values and explore 3NTthree no trump rather than rushing into a minor-suit contract.

With fewer values and sufficient support, jump directly to the three level to make it harder for the opponents to enter. Inverted Minors therefore serves two purposes in bridge bidding: space for our own exploration and less space for the opponents.

All these responses deny a four-card major. The lengths refer to responder's cards in opener's suit, not the combined length of the two hands.

Some partnerships allow a single diamond raise with four-card support, depending on the minimum length promised by 1♦one diamond. This requires a separate agreement. Our examples and exercises use at least five-card support for minor-suit raises.

10+ has no upper limit. The same initial response can show an invitation or enough strength for game and possibly slam. Opener must not pass 2♣two clubs or 2♦two diamonds, but the partnership is not yet committed to game.

Examples

In these examples, partner has opened and the right-hand opponent has passed.

1. An invitational hand with clubs

After a 1♣one club opening, we hold:

♠ A84spades: ace, 8, 4.
♥ K72hearts: king, 7, 2.
♦ 85diamonds: 8, 5.
♣ QJ963clubs: queen, jack, 9, 6, 3.

We bid 2♣two clubs. We have 10 HCP, five clubs and no four-card major. We make the strong single raise, leaving opener room to describe the hand. We do not bid 3♣three clubs, which would show 6–9 points in our agreement.

2. A strong hand with diamonds

After a 1♦one diamond opening, we hold:

♠ K84spades: king, 8, 4.
♥ A7hearts: ace, 7.
♦ AQ963diamonds: ace, queen, 9, 6, 3.
♣ J85clubs: jack, 8, 5.

We bid 2♦two diamonds. Our 14 HCP are not too much for this response: 2♦two diamonds has no upper limit. We first show support and will then ensure that the auction reaches at least game.

3. A jump raise

After a 1♦one diamond opening, we hold:

♠ 84spades: 8, 4.
♥ Q72hearts: queen, 7, 2.
♦ KJ963diamonds: king, jack, 9, 6, 3.
♣ J85clubs: jack, 8, 5.

We bid 3♦three diamonds. We have 7 HCP and five-card support. The jump describes our limited hand and makes life harder for the opponents. Opener may pass.

4. The major comes first

After a 1♣one club opening, we hold:

♠ KJ84spades: king, jack, 8, 4.
♥ A72hearts: ace, 7, 2.
♦ 8diamonds: 8.
♣ Q9632clubs: queen, 9, 6, 3, 2.

We bid 1♠one spade. Although we have 10 HCP and five clubs, searching for a spade fit takes priority. An immediate 2♣two clubs would conceal our four-card major.

How do we continue?

The following continuations assume that the opponents keep passing. They follow the strong single raise 1♣one club–2♣two clubs or 1♦one diamond–2♦two diamonds.

A stopper is a holding that prevents the opponents from immediately cashing all their tricks in a suit at notrump. It is not the same as length: we can have a stopper without four cards, and four cards without a secure stopper.

When several suits are available at the two level, show the cheapest stopper. After 1♣one club–2♣two clubs, 2♦two diamonds shows diamond values. 2♥two hearts or 2♠two spades shows values in that major. We are not searching for a 4–4 major fit, since responder has already denied a four-card major.

2NTtwo no trump guarantees stoppers in both majors, not necessarily in the other minor. Check that suit too before choosing 3NTthree no trump.

Responder's continuation

Responder knows whether the original raise was invitational or stronger. With the minimum of the 10+ range, responder may pass 2NTtwo no trump or three of the agreed minor. With game values, continue bidding.

Do not pass opener's new suit. Add information about our stoppers or choose notrump when the combined strength and coverage of the other suits are sufficient. In our simple version, 2NTtwo no trump or three of the agreed minor may be passed unless a game force has already been established. Other continuations need appropriate strength and clear agreements.

1. Stopping in clubs

Our partnership auction is 1♣one club–2♣two clubs–3♣three clubs. As responder, we hold:

♠ A84spades: ace, 8, 4.
♥ K72hearts: king, 7, 2.
♦ 85diamonds: 8, 5.
♣ QJ963clubs: queen, jack, 9, 6, 3.

Pass. We have 10 HCP and opener has shown a minimum. Our initial 2♣two clubs required one further bid, not a game contract.

2. Stopping in notrump

Our partnership auction is 1♦one diamond–2♦two diamonds–2NTtwo no trump. As responder, we hold:

♠ 84spades: 8, 4.
♥ K72hearts: king, 7, 2.
♦ KJ963diamonds: king, jack, 9, 6, 3.
♣ K85clubs: king, 8, 5.

Pass. We have 10 HCP and opener has shown a balanced minimum. Our spades are not a problem because 2NTtwo no trump promises stoppers in both majors. In clubs we hold the king with two small cards.

3. Bidding game

Our partnership auction is 1♣one club–2♣two clubs–2♥two hearts. As responder, we hold:

♠ AQ4spades: ace, queen, 4.
♥ 83hearts: 8, 3.
♦ KJ5diamonds: king, jack, 5.
♣ K9632clubs: king, 9, 6, 3, 2.

We bid 3NTthree no trump. We have 13 HCP, club support and stoppers in spades and diamonds. Opener showed a heart stopper. The 2♥two hearts bid did not promise four hearts or ask for heart support.

Points to remember

  • The raise to the two level is stronger than the jump raise. Opener must not pass the initial 2♣two clubs or 2♦two diamonds.
  • Forcing for one round does not mean forcing to game. Our version allows us to play 2NTtwo no trump or three of the agreed minor.
  • A four-card major comes first, even with sufficient support for the minor.
  • The new-suit bids discussed here show stoppers or concentrated values, without promising four cards.
  • Before bidding 3NTthree no trump, consider the combined strength and stoppers, including the other minor.
  • 6–9 points for a jump raise is the choice in this lesson. Other versions use weaker hands. Agree which version your partnership plays.
  • Use after an overcall, double or an initial pass requires separate agreements and is outside this lesson.
  • Agree the strength ranges, minimum support length and continuations. Do not automatically import meanings from another version of Inverted Minors.
