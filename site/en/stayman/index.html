Stayman

Application, examples and responses

A 1NT opening describes strength and a balanced hand, but leaves a possible four-card major unknown. Stayman looks for a major-suit fit before we choose notrump.

When is it used?

Here, partner opens 1NT with 15–17 high-card points, without opposing intervention. We hold at least one four-card major and at least 8 high-card points. Our artificial 2♣ asks about a four-card major; it says nothing about clubs and forces opener to reply.Here, partner opens one no trump with 15–17 high-card points, without opposing intervention. We hold at least one four-card major and at least 8 high-card points. Our artificial two clubs asks about a four-card major; it says nothing about clubs and forces opener to reply.

Note: if a partnership uses a direct 2NT response as a Transfer to a minor, an invitation in notrump goes through Stayman. Under that agreement, 2♣ need not promise a four-card major. Transfers to the minors will be our next lesson.Note: if a partnership uses a direct two no trump response as a Transfer to a minor, an invitation in notrump goes through Stayman. Under that agreement, two clubs need not promise a four-card major. Transfers to the minors will be our next lesson.

This lesson uses basic Stayman. Special weak-hand applications require suitable distribution and agreed continuations; they are outside its scope.

What does it achieve?

A 4–4 major-suit fit may play better than notrump: trumps allow ruffs and help us use both hands' distribution. Stayman leaves room to decide the denomination and level.

Normally, 8–9 high-card points invite game and 10 or more suggest game opposite 15–17. Distribution and card quality also count; these are guidelines.

How does responder continue?

With a fit, 8–9 high-card points invite with 3♥ or 3♠, which opener may pass. With 10 or more and no slam interest, bid 4♥ or 4♠ to play.With a fit, 8–9 high-card points invite with three hearts or three spades, which opener may pass. With 10 or more and no slam interest, bid four hearts or four spades to play.

Opener usually declines with a minimum and accepts with a maximum. With an intermediate hand, consider distribution, useful honours and overall quality.

Points to remember

2♣ asks about a major and forces one round, not game. 2♦ denies a four-card major and is not natural; 2♥ does not deny four spades. Points alone do not justify basic Stayman. With one five-card or longer major and fewer than four in the other, normally use two clubs asks about a major and forces one round, not game. two diamonds denies a four-card major and is not natural; two hearts does not deny four spades. Points alone do not justify basic Stayman. With one five-card or longer major and fewer than four in the other, normally use Jacoby Transfer. Opener's initial reply does not distinguish strength within 15–17.
