# Stayman

A 1NT opening describes strength and a balanced hand, but leaves a possible four-card major unknown. Stayman looks for a major-suit fit before we choose notrump.

## When is it used?

Here, partner opens 1NT with 15–17 high-card points, without opposing intervention. We hold at least one four-card major and at least 8 high-card points. Our artificial 2♣ asks about a four-card major; it says nothing about clubs and forces opener to reply.

Note: if a partnership uses a direct 2NT response as a Transfer to a minor, an invitation in notrump goes through Stayman. Under that agreement, 2♣ need not promise a four-card major. Transfers to the minors will be our next lesson.

This lesson uses basic Stayman. Special weak-hand applications require suitable distribution and agreed continuations; they are outside its scope.

## What does it achieve?

A 4–4 major-suit fit may play better than notrump: trumps allow ruffs and help us use both hands' distribution. Stayman leaves room to decide the denomination and level.

Normally, 8–9 high-card points invite game and 10 or more suggest game opposite 15–17. Distribution and card quality also count; these are guidelines.

## Examples of the initial response

♠ KJ84  
♥ Q732  
♦ K65  
♣ 92

We have 9 high-card points. We have four cards in each major. Bid 2♣ Stayman to look for a fit before inviting game; we are not yet committed to game.

♠ AQ84  
♥ K732  
♦ Q65  
♣ 92

We have 11 high-card points. We have four cards in each major. Bid 2♣ Stayman before choosing a game. With a fit, choose the major; without one, the basic continuation is 3NT.

## How does opener respond?

| Stayman | How does opener respond? |
| --- | --- |
| **2♦** | No four-card major; does not promise diamonds. |
| **2♥** | Four hearts; may also have four spades. |
| **2♠** | Four spades; denies four hearts. |

With both four-card majors, opener bids 2♥ first. These replies describe the majors, not minimum or maximum strength.

♠ AQ8  
♥ K72  
♦ KJ65  
♣ Q92

After opening 1NT with 15 high-card points, reply 2♦ to Stayman: there is no four-card major. The diamonds in this hand are incidental; 2♦ does not promise diamonds.

♠ KJ84  
♥ AQ73  
♦ K65  
♣ Q2

With 15 high-card points and both four-card majors, reply 2♥ first. Spades remain possible and may be found later; maximum strength does not justify a jump.

♠ AQ84  
♥ K72  
♦ QJ6  
♣ K92

With 15 high-card points, four spades and only three hearts, reply 2♠. This shows spades and denies four hearts.

## How does responder continue?

With a fit, 8–9 high-card points invite with 3♥ or 3♠, which opener may pass. With 10 or more and no slam interest, bid 4♥ or 4♠ to play.

Opener usually declines with a minimum and accepts with a maximum. With an intermediate hand, consider distribution, useful honours and overall quality.

♠ Q84  
♥ KJ73  
♦ K65  
♣ 972

After 1NT – 2♣ – 2♥, 9 high-card points and four hearts justify 3♥, inviting game. Opener makes the final decision.

♠ K84  
♥ AQ73  
♦ Q65  
♣ J92

With 12 high-card points and four-card support in hearts, bid 4♥ to play. There is enough for game even opposite opener's minimum, without slam interest.

Without a major-suit fit, 8–9 high-card points invite with 2NT; with 10 or more and no slam interest, the basic game choice is 3NT.

These notrump continuations assume a suitable hand. Hands with 5–4 in the majors and their special continuations require further agreements and are outside this basic lesson.

♠ KJ84  
♥ Q7  
♦ K865  
♣ 972

After 1NT – 2♣ – 2♦, we have 9 high-card points. Opener denied a four-card major, so there is no spade fit. Bid 2NT to invite; opener may pass with a minimum or bid 3NT with a suitable maximum.

♠ AQ84  
♥ K7  
♦ Q865  
♣ J92

After 1NT – 2♣ – 2♥, we have 12 high-card points, four spades and only two hearts. Bid 3NT. Because we used Stayman but did not support hearts, opener infers four spades: with four spades, choose 4♠; otherwise pass 3NT.

In the basic version, where Stayman promises a four-card major, after 1NT – 2♣ – 2♥ – 2NT opener with four spades bids 3♠ with a minimum, passable, or 4♠ to accept. Without four spades, choose pass or 3NT. This inference is not automatic under the minor-Transfer agreement noted above.

## Points to remember

2♣ asks about a major and forces one round, not game. 2♦ denies a four-card major and is not natural; 2♥ does not deny four spades. Points alone do not justify basic Stayman. With one five-card or longer major and fewer than four in the other, normally use [Jacoby Transfer](../jacoby-transfer/). Opener's initial reply does not distinguish strength within 15–17.
