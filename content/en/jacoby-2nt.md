# Jacoby 2NT

## When does it apply?

**Jacoby 2NT** applies after **1♥ or 1♠** promising at least five cards, with **at least four-card support and game-going strength**. This lesson covers a first- or second-seat opening, an unpassed responder and no opposing interference.

## What does it achieve?

We want to show a strong fit immediately while preserving room to explore slam. The artificial **2NT response is game forcing**: opener must not pass, and the partnership must reach at least game. Opener's subsequent bids describe strength and distribution, helping us decide whether game is enough or the hands fit well enough for slam.

## What does 2NT show?

| Bid | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1♥ – 2NT | At least four-card support for the major opened, with game-going strength. |
| 1♠ – 2NT | At least four-card support for the major opened, with game-going strength. |

Assess game values using high-card points and distribution. There is **no upper strength limit**. The response **does not promise a balanced hand or stoppers in the other suits**; it shows a strong major-suit fit. With only three-card support, use another forcing response and show support later.

## Examples

### 1. Strong heart support

Partner opens **1♥** and right-hand opponent passes.

♠ AQ3  
♥ KQ74  
♦ A85  
♣ J96

Bid **2NT Jacoby**: 16 high-card points and four hearts. Show strength and support immediately, leaving partner room to describe the hand. Our balanced shape does not mean we must play notrump.

### 2. Very strong spade support

Partner opens **1♠** and right-hand opponent passes.

♠ AKJ7  
♥ A8  
♦ KQ6  
♣ K952

Bid **2NT Jacoby**: 20 high-card points and four spades. Slam is very interesting, but we need more information first. Jacoby is not restricted to hands satisfied with game; it can start exploration of much higher contracts.

## How do we continue?

Throughout these continuations the opponents pass. In our classical version, opener's rebids are:

| Bid | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A new suit at the three level | A singleton or void in that suit; strength is not yet defined. |
| Three of the opening major | Strong, about 18+ points, without a singleton or void. |
| 3NT | Intermediate, about 15–17 points, without a singleton or void. |
| Four of the opening major | A minimum opening without a singleton or void. |
| A new suit at the four level | A good second suit of at least five cards, a source of extra tricks. |

These strength ranges are approximate; distribution and honour quality matter. Other versions exist. Shortness helps responder reassess: small cards may be covered by ruffs, whereas a king or queen opposite shortness may be less useful. **The direct jump to game is the weakest rebid**; stronger hands leave bidding space for slam exploration.

For each of the next five hands, we open **1♠** and partner responds **2NT Jacoby**.

### 1. A singleton

♠ AQJ84  
♥ 7  
♦ KJ63  
♣ A85

Bid **3♥**, showing a singleton or void in hearts. This does not show heart length or define our strength yet. Partner can now judge how useful their cards are opposite this shape.

### 2. A minimum without shortness

♠ KQJ84  
♥ K72  
♦ Q63  
♣ J5

Bid **4♠**. With 12 high-card points and no special distributional assets, we have a minimum opening. Partner has already committed us to game; we simply describe our minimum.

### 3. A strong hand without shortness

♠ AKJ84  
♥ AQ7  
♦ K63  
♣ K5

Bid **3♠**. With 20 high-card points we preserve room to explore slam. Although lower, this bid is stronger than the direct 4♠ and cannot be passed.

### 4. Intermediate strength without shortness

♠ AQJ864  
♥ K7  
♦ QJ3  
♣ K5

Bid **3NT**. With 16 high-card points and no singleton or void, we describe intermediate strength. We are not suggesting abandoning the agreed spade fit.

### 5. A good second five-card suit

♠ AKJ84  
♥ Q7  
♦ 6  
♣ KQJ85

Bid **4♣**, showing a good second five-card suit that may supply extra tricks for slam. In our version, describing such a good second suit takes priority over showing the singleton: choose 4♣ rather than 3♦. This is **not merely a club control-bid**.

### How does responder continue?

Reassess after opener's description. Without slam prospects, bid **four of the agreed major**, or pass if opener has already bid game showing a minimum. A very strong responder may continue even over that minimum: Jacoby imposed no upper limit.

With slam interest, use **control-bids**. The major is agreed, so another suit shows an ace, king, singleton or void according to partnership agreements; it is not a search for a new fit.

Ask for aces or key cards when enough is known to judge slam. **Do not bid 4NT automatically just because you have many points**: first consider controls, trump quality and how the hands fit together.

### Reassessing after shortness

In both examples, partner opens **1♠**, we respond **2NT Jacoby**, and partner rebids **3♥**, showing a singleton or void in hearts.

### 1. Useful honours outside the short suit

♠ AQ74  
♥ 864  
♦ KQ5  
♣ A83

Heart shortness improves our hand: our three small hearts can be covered by ruffs, and our honours are elsewhere. Bid **4♣**, showing a club control and slam interest. This promises neither club length nor a final contract yet.

### 2. Honours concentrated opposite shortness

♠ QJ74  
♥ KQJ  
♦ K85  
♣ J63

Bid **4♠**. We have game values, but much of our strength is in hearts, opposite partner's singleton or void. Those honours fit the distribution less effectively, and our outside assets are limited. With the information available, **we do not encourage further slam exploration**.

## Points to remember

Jacoby 2NT and [Bergen Raises](../bergen-raises/) complement each other. With four or more trumps, Bergen covers weak, constructive and invitational hands in our version; Jacoby covers game-going hands or better.

Do not confuse **Jacoby 2NT with Jacoby transfers**: here 2NT responds to 1♥ or 1♠ and shows a strong fit.

In our version it is off by a passed hand or after an opposing double or overcall. Interference **after** our 2NT does not cancel the game force, but continuations need separate agreement.

Agree the rebids before playing the convention, especially **3NT and new suits at the four level**, whose meanings vary between versions.
