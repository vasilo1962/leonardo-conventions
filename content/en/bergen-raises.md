# Bergen Raises

## When do they apply?

**Bergen Raises** apply after a **1♥ or 1♠** opening promising at least five cards, when responder has **at least four-card support** for opener's major.

This lesson covers an unpassed responder and no opposing interference. Many partnerships also retain Bergen after a double or a one-level overcall, provided the Bergen bids remain available. This requires explicit partnership agreement.

## What do they achieve?

Once a major-suit fit is established, opener needs to know both responder's strength and the length of the support.

Bergen distinguishes three types of hands with four or more trumps: weak, constructive and invitational.

Reaching the three level quickly, with at least nine combined trumps, takes away opponents' bidding space while helping partner choose the final contract. In bridge, this combines describing the fit with obstructing the opponents.

## What does each bid mean?

After **1♥ or 1♠**:

| Our response | Major-suit support | High-card points | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3♣ | At least four cards | 7–9 | Constructive raise |
| 3♦ | At least four cards | 10–12 | Game invitation |
| 3♥ /♠ | At least four cards | Up to 6 | Weak, preemptive jump raise |

**3♥ /♠** means 3♥ after 1♥ and 3♠ after 1♠.

**3♣ and 3♦ are artificial.** They show support for partner's major, not length in the minor bid. **Opener must not pass either response.**

The direct raise to three of the major is passable. With a very weak hand, consider vulnerability and distribution carefully.

These point ranges are this lesson's agreement. Distribution and honour quality also matter in hand evaluation.

## Examples

In these examples partner opens and right-hand opponent passes.

### 1. A constructive raise

After **1♠**, we hold:

♠ K842  
♥ A73  
♦ 85  
♣ J963

We bid **3♣**: 8 HCP and four-card spade support, describing strength and fit without promising clubs.

### 2. A game invitation

After **1♥**, we hold:

♠ A83  
♥ KJ74  
♦ K85  
♣ 963

We bid **3♦**: 11 HCP and four-card heart support. We invite game and let opener decide according to their hand.

### 3. A weak jump raise

After **1♠**, we hold:

♠ Q974  
♥ 83  
♦ K852  
♣ 963

We bid **3♠**: 5 HCP and four-card support. This is a preempt, not a game invitation. Partner may pass.

### 4. Only three-card support

After **1♥**, we hold:

♠ K83  
♥ Q72  
♦ A854  
♣ 963

We bid **2♥**. There are 9 HCP but only three hearts. Bergen 3♣ would promise at least four-card support.

## How do we continue?

Opener now knows there are at least nine combined trumps. To stop or bid game, opener considers partner's shown strength, their own distribution and honour quality. The following continuations assume the opponents pass throughout.

### After 3♣ — constructive

| Opener's continuation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 3♥ /♠ | To play. Responder passes. |
| 4♥ /♠ | Bids game. |
| 3♦ | Invites game, asking whether responder is minimum or maximum. |

For the **3♦** enquiry we use this simple agreement:

- With **7–8 points**, responder returns to **3♥ /♠**.
- With **9 points**, responder bids **4♥ /♠**.

The enquiry does not promise diamonds. This minimum/maximum distinction belongs to the simple version taught here.

**Notice who bids 3♦:** as the initial response it shows the 10–12-point raise; as opener's rebid after 3♣ it is the enquiry above.

### After 3♦ — invitational

Responder has shown 10–12 points and at least four-card support. Opener chooses:

| Continuation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 3♥ /♠ | Declines the invitation. Responder passes. |
| 4♥ /♠ | Accepts the invitation and bids game. |

Do not rely solely on the combined point count. An extra trump, useful shortness or a good side suit can make game more attractive.

### After a direct 3♥ /♠ — weak jump raise

Opener passes with an ordinary opening hand. Partner has preempted, not invited game.

To continue to game requires considerable extra playing strength, remembering partner may have very few points.

### 1. Declining the invitation

We opened **1♥** and partner responded **3♦**. We hold:

♠ K83  
♥ AQ742  
♦ K85  
♣ 96

We bid **3♥**. With 12 HCP, a balanced hand and no extra values, we decline. Partner passes.

### 2. Accepting the invitation

We opened **1♥** and partner responded **3♦**. We hold:

♠ A83  
♥ KQJ74  
♦ AQ5  
♣ 96

We bid **4♥**. With 16 HCP, a good trump suit and at least four-card support opposite, we accept the game invitation.

### 3. Answering the enquiry with a minimum

Our partnership's auction is **1♠–3♣–3♦**. As responder we hold:

♠ K842  
♥ Q73  
♦ 85  
♣ Q963

We bid **3♠**. Our 7 HCP are the minimum for our constructive response, so we decline the game invitation.

### 4. Answering the enquiry with a maximum

Our partnership's auction is **1♠–3♣–3♦**. As responder we hold:

♠ K842  
♥ A73  
♦ 85  
♣ Q963

We bid **4♠**. Our 9 HCP are the maximum for our constructive response, so we accept opener's invitation.

## Points to remember

- **Bergen requires at least four-card support.** With three trumps, follow your other system agreements.
- **3♣ and 3♦ do not show minor-suit length.** Opener must not pass.
- **A direct 3♥ /♠ is preemptive**, not invitational.
- **With game-forcing strength, do not limit the hand with 3♣ or 3♦.** Use your agreed strong raise, such as [Jacoby 2NT](../jacoby-2nt/) if you play it.
- **Point ranges vary between versions.** Here constructive 3♣ shows 7–9 and invitational 3♦ shows 10–12.
- **Reverse Bergen swaps the meanings of 3♣ and 3♦.** Know which version you play.
- **After a double or a one-level overcall**, retain Bergen only by agreement and when the bids remain available.
- **For third- or fourth-seat openings**, using Bergen requires a separate agreement, particularly when the partnership uses [Reverse Drury](../drury/).
