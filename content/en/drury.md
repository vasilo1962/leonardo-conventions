# Drury

## When does it apply?

Use **Drury** after you have passed and partner opens **1♥ or 1♠ in third or fourth seat**. In this lesson the opening promises at least five cards and opponents do not overcall. With invitational values and three-card or longer support, respond **2♣**, an artificial bid unrelated to clubs. We first explain **Reverse Drury**, then **Two-way Drury**, which distinguishes three-card support from longer support.

## What is it for?

Especially in third seat, partner may open light. An invitational jump raise could take the partnership too high. Drury shows strength and support economically: opener may rebid two of the major to play, or explore game when justified.

## What does 2♣ show?

| Response | Support | Strength and status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2♣ Drury | At least three cards in opener's major | Invitational; artificial and forcing one round |

You invite game opposite a normal opening. Evaluate high-card points, distribution and fit quality; your previous pass limits your strength. **2♣ does not promise clubs and opener must not pass.** With a weaker supporting hand make a simple raise to two of the major. Without three-card support, do not use Drury.

## Examples of the Drury response

In these examples you have passed, partner opens in third or fourth seat and opponents pass.

### 1. Three-card support

Partner opens **1♠**:

♠ K83  
♥ A74  
♦ Q962  
♣ Q85

Bid **2♣ Drury**. With 11 HCP and three-card support, invite game while retaining the option of stopping in 2♠ opposite a light opening.

### 2. Four-card support

Partner opens **1♥**:

♠ AQ84  
♥ K963  
♦ Q74  
♣ 85

Bid **2♣ Drury**. You have 11 HCP, four-card support and a club doubleton. Simple Reverse Drury includes this hand; it is not restricted to three-card support.

### 3. A weaker hand

Partner opens **1♠**:

♠ K83  
♥ Q74  
♦ J962  
♣ 853

Bid **2♠**, a simple raise. You have a fit, but 6 HCP and a balanced shape do not justify a game invitation.

## How does the bidding continue?

Opponents pass throughout the following auctions.

### Opener's rebid in Reverse Drury

After **2♣ Drury**, opener knows partner has support and invitational strength. In our version:

| Rebid | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Two of the opening major | No game interest; to play |
| 2♦ | Artificial; a normal opening with game interest, but no final decision. Does not promise diamonds; forcing one round |
| Four of the opening major | Accepts the invitation and bids game |

**Two of the opening major is to play. Responder passes.** Opener may have opened light or hold a minimum that does not justify further bidding. **2♦** leaves room to clarify responder's hand; it does not force game. Assess both hands together.

### 1. Stopping low

You opened **1♠ in third seat** and partner responded **2♣ Drury**:

♠ KQ874  
♥ 83  
♦ A62  
♣ J95

Bid **2♠**. With a light 10-HCP opening you do not want to explore game. Partner passes.

### 2. Asking for a better description

You opened **1♠ in third seat** and partner responded **2♣ Drury**:

♠ KQ874  
♥ A7  
♦ K62  
♣ J95

Bid **2♦**. Your 13 HCP make a normal opening, but game depends on partner's hand. This artificial bid does not show your three diamonds.

Our simple continuation: responder bids two of the major with a **minimum Drury**, and game with a **maximum and a suitable hand**. Four-card support, useful shortage and good honours can improve the evaluation; points alone do not decide. This is the lesson's agreement; more detailed methods exist.

### 3. Bidding game

You opened **1♠ in third seat** and partner responded **2♣ Drury**:

♠ AQJ84  
♥ K7  
♦ A62  
♣ Q95

Bid **4♠**. With 16 HCP, a good five-card major and partner's invitational support, accept directly. There is no need to bid 2♦ first.

### Connection with Bergen Raises

Some partnerships also play [Bergen Raises](../bergen-raises/) over third- and fourth-seat openings. If Bergen already shows invitational hands with at least four-card support, they may reserve **2♣ Reverse Drury for exactly three-card support**, without needing Two-way Drury.

That is a separate partnership agreement. In this lesson's simple version, **2♣ promises at least three-card support**.

### Two-way Drury

Simple Drury does not tell opener whether the partnership has an eight-card or longer fit, which may affect the game decision. **Two-way Drury** distinguishes the cases:

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 2♣ | Invitational, exactly three-card support |
| 2♦ | Invitational, at least four-card support |

Both are **artificial and forcing one round**, not natural minor-suit bids. You must still have passed before partner's third- or fourth-seat 1♥/1♠ opening.

### Opener's rebid in Two-way

- **Two of the opening major is to play**; responder passes.
- **Four of the major** accepts the invitation and bids game.

After **2♣**, opener's **2♦** enquiry remains available. After a **2♦ response**, opener already knows of four-card or longer support, and that enquiry is unavailable. Other invitational continuations require a separate agreement. The cost of Two-way is losing the natural **2♦ response** as well as the natural **2♣ response**.

### Two-way example — exactly three-card support

You have passed; partner opens **1♠ in third or fourth seat** and opponents pass:

♠ KQ7  
♥ A84  
♦ Q962  
♣ 853

Bid **2♣ Drury**. With 11 HCP and exactly three trumps, show invitational strength and the precise support length.

### Two-way example — four-card support

You have passed; partner opens **1♠ in third or fourth seat** and opponents pass:

♠ KQ74  
♥ A83  
♦ Q962  
♣ 85

Bid **2♦ Drury**. Again 11 HCP, but with four-card support and a club doubleton. Opposite the promised five-card opening, opener immediately knows of at least a nine-card fit. Simple Drury would use **2♣ with both hands**; Two-way separates them with **2♣ and 2♦**.

## Points to remember

- **A previous pass is essential:** Drury is a passed-hand response to partner's third- or fourth-seat 1♥/1♠ opening.
- **A fit is essential:** a good hand without at least three-card support is insufficient.
- **In this lesson Drury is off after an opposing double or overcall.** Use your other partnership agreements.
- **Two of the major is to play.** You have already shown invitational strength; pass.
- **Agree on the version:** simple 2♣ shows three-plus; Two-way 2♣ exactly three and 2♦ four-plus.
- **Agree on Bergen too:** its use after third- and fourth-seat openings needs a separate agreement, particularly alongside Drury.
