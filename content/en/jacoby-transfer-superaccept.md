# Jacoby Transfer – Superaccepts

After [Jacoby Transfer](../jacoby-transfer/), opener can show **17 HCP and four-card support** by superaccepting. Responder then knows of at least a nine-card fit and can reassess whether to stop, bid game or explore slam.

## When does it apply?

We assume a balanced **1NT opening with 15–17 HCP**, no opposing intervention. In this version every superaccept requires 17 HCP and exactly four cards in responder’s major. Otherwise accept at the two level; five-card support is outside this lesson.

## What does it achieve?

A nine-card fit can provide extra tricks. Knowing opener’s doubleton helps responder judge ruffing values in the shorter trump hand and the usefulness of queens and jacks opposite shortness. Consider trump quality and the whole hand.

**A superaccept does not force game.** The initial Transfer can be made with zero HCP, so we preserve a way to stop at the three level.

## How do we superaccept?

Bid the doubleton suit as cheaply as possible. If the doubleton is in the suit of the **Transfer bid**—diamonds after 2♦ or hearts after 2♥—bid **2NT** instead. Without a doubleton, with 4333, bid three of the major. Every entry below shows 17 HCP and four-card support.

| Feature | After 1NT – 2♦ | After 1NT – 2♥ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doubleton: clubs | 3♣ | 3♣ |
| Doubleton: diamonds | 2NT | 3♦ |
| Doubleton: hearts | — | 2NT |
| Doubleton: spades | 2♠ | — |
| 4333, no doubleton | 3♥ | 3♠ |

2NT leaves 3♦ or 3♥ available as a retransfer. If opener bid 3♦ to show doubleton diamonds after 2♦, weak responder would have to bid 3♥ personally to stop low. Using 2NT keeps opener as declarer. The same principle applies to spades with doubleton hearts.

Superaccepts in a new suit or 2NT are **artificial and forcing for one round**. Three of the major showing 4333 **may be passed**.

## Decide as opener

### 1. 3♣

♠ KQ7  
♥ AJ84  
♦ KJ62  
♣ K5

After **1NT – 2♦**, bid **3♣**: 17 HCP, four hearts and doubleton clubs. This shows shortness, not a long club suit. Responder may bid 3♦ to retransfer to 3♥.

### 2. 2NT

♠ AQ7  
♥ KJ84  
♦ A5  
♣ K762

After **1NT – 2♦**, bid **2NT**: 17 HCP, four hearts and doubleton diamonds. This preserves 3♦ as a retransfer. The ace in the doubleton does not prevent showing it.

### 3. 2NT

♠ KJ84  
♥ A5  
♦ AQ7  
♣ K762

After **1NT – 2♥**, bid **2NT**: 17 HCP, four spades and doubleton hearts. Responder can use 3♥ to play 3♠ or 4♥ to play 4♠.

### 4. 3♠

♠ AQ84  
♥ K72  
♦ AJ6  
♣ K85

After **1NT – 2♥**, bid **3♠**: 17 HCP, four spades and 4333, without a doubleton. Responder knows there are no doubleton ruffing values and may pass.

### 5. 2♥

♠ KQ7  
♥ AJ84  
♦ K862  
♣ K5

After **1NT – 2♦**, bid **2♥**: with 16 HCP, four hearts and a doubleton, we still lack the agreed 17 HCP. Simple acceptance does not deny four-card support.

## Continue as responder

**With a weak hand**, retransfer at the three level—3♦ for hearts, 3♥ for spades—and pass opener’s acceptance. If opener has already bid three of the major with 4333, pass directly.

♠ J85  
♥ QT963  
♦ 864  
♣ 98

After **1NT – 2♦ – 2NT**, opener shows 17 HCP, four hearts and doubleton diamonds. With 3 HCP, bid **3♦**, then pass **3♥**. The retransfer is artificial.

**With game values and no slam interest**, retransfer at the four level: 4♦ for 4♥, 4♥ for 4♠, then pass. If opener has already bid three of the major with 4333, raise directly to four.

♠ KJ975  
♥ A6  
♦ Q84  
♣ 962

After **1NT – 2♥ – 3♣**, opener shows 17 HCP, four spades and doubleton clubs. With 10 HCP, bid **4♥**, then pass **4♠**, keeping opener as declarer.

**With slam interest**, prefer the cheapest available control-bid below game. Show first- or second-round control: ace or void, king or singleton. Retransfers remain reserved for that purpose, not control-bids. You may retransfer at the three level and then show a control.

♠ AKJ75  
♥ 84  
♦ KQ6  
♣ A72

After **1NT – 2♥ – 3♦**, with 17 HCP bid **4♣**, showing a club control. Opener’s 3♦ showed a doubleton, not a control. We control diamonds but not hearts, so need information about hearts before asking for keycards.

With enough strength and trick potential for slam, and all side-suit controls established, we may ask for aces or keycards according to partnership agreement. Control-bids need not repeat information already known.

♠ KQJ75  
♥ A6  
♦ K84  
♣ A92

After **1NT – 2♥ – 3♣**, with 17 HCP, strong spades and all side suits controlled, **4NT** may ask for aces or keycards with spades as trumps. Alternatively retransfer with 3♥, then ask after 3♠, retaining opener as declarer.

## Points to remember

The initial Transfer promises no points. A new suit by opener shows a doubleton, not length or a control. Here 2NT is artificial, not a natural invitation. A retransfer is not a control-bid. Asking for aces does not replace evaluating tricks, side controls and trumps. Other methods use different meanings; agree the version first.

### Sources and the version taught

The doubleton method and 2NT preserving a retransfer follow **[Byron Lund](https://www.abf.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/How-to-Play-Expert-Bridge.pdf#page=87)**, *How to Play Expert Bridge*, pages 82–90. Lund suggests three of the major with 4333 and 18 HCP, downgraded for shape; **our lesson uses 17 HCP for 4333 too**. Responder’s continuations are our stated agreements, not every extra treatment in the book.

**[Robert S. Todd](https://www.advinbridge.com/this-week-in-bridge/518)** supports the retransfer principle; his superaccept ranges and meanings differ. **[Larry Cohen](https://larryco.com/bridge-articles/control-bidding?p=true)** explains economical first- or second-round control-bidding and when to use Blackwood. The text and example hands were created for Leonardo Bridge.
