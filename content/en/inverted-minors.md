# Inverted Minors

## When does it apply?

**Inverted Minors** applies after partner's natural **1♣ or 1♦ opening**, when the opponents have neither overcalled nor doubled.

Here we use the version in which a single raise shows **10+ high-card points** and is **forcing for one round**, while a jump raise shows **6–9 high-card points** and may be passed. This is distinct from Two Way Inverted Minors.

Responder has no four-card major. With four hearts or four spades, respond in the major first so that a possible fit there is not lost.

## What is its purpose?

“Inverted” reverses the usual strength relationship between the single raise and the jump raise: **raising to the two level is stronger than jumping to the three level**.

With invitational or stronger values, keep the bidding low. This leaves room to describe our values and explore 3NT rather than rushing into a minor-suit contract.

With fewer values and sufficient support, jump directly to the three level to make it harder for the opponents to enter. Inverted Minors therefore serves two purposes in bridge bidding: space for our own exploration and less space for the opponents.

## What does each bid mean?

| Partner opens | Our response | Strength | Support | Forcing status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1♣ | 2♣ | 10+ HCP | At least five clubs | Forcing for one round |
| 1♦ | 2♦ | 10+ HCP | Usually at least five diamonds | Forcing for one round |
| 1♣ | 3♣ | 6–9 HCP | At least five clubs | May be passed |
| 1♦ | 3♦ | 6–9 HCP | At least five diamonds | May be passed |

All these responses deny a four-card major. The lengths refer to responder's cards in opener's suit, not the combined length of the two hands.

Some partnerships allow a single diamond raise with four-card support, depending on the minimum length promised by 1♦. This requires a separate agreement. Our examples and exercises use at least five-card support for minor-suit raises.

**10+ has no upper limit**. The same initial response can show an invitation or enough strength for game and possibly slam. Opener must not pass 2♣ or 2♦, but the partnership is not yet committed to game.

## Examples

In these examples, partner has opened and the right-hand opponent has passed.

### 1. An invitational hand with clubs

After a **1♣** opening, we hold:

♠ A84  
♥ K72  
♦ 85  
♣ QJ963

We bid **2♣**. We have 10 HCP, five clubs and no four-card major. We make the strong single raise, leaving opener room to describe the hand. We do not bid 3♣, which would show 6–9 points in our agreement.

### 2. A strong hand with diamonds

After a **1♦** opening, we hold:

♠ K84  
♥ A7  
♦ AQ963  
♣ J85

We bid **2♦**. Our 14 HCP are not too much for this response: 2♦ has no upper limit. We first show support and will then ensure that the auction reaches at least game.

### 3. A jump raise

After a **1♦** opening, we hold:

♠ 84  
♥ Q72  
♦ KJ963  
♣ J85

We bid **3♦**. We have 7 HCP and five-card support. The jump describes our limited hand and makes life harder for the opponents. Opener may pass.

### 4. The major comes first

After a **1♣** opening, we hold:

♠ KJ84  
♥ A72  
♦ 8  
♣ Q9632

We bid **1♠**. Although we have 10 HCP and five clubs, searching for a spade fit takes priority. An immediate 2♣ would conceal our four-card major.

## How do we continue?

The following continuations assume that the opponents keep passing. They follow the strong single raise **1♣–2♣ or 1♦–2♦**.

### Opener's rebid

| Opener's rebid | Meaning in our version | May it be passed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2NT | Balanced, 12–14 HCP, stoppers in both majors | Yes |
| 3♣ after 1♣–2♣, or 3♦ after 1♦–2♦ | A minimum opening without a more suitable descriptive bid | Yes |
| A new suit at the two level | A stopper or concentrated values in the suit; does not promise four cards | No, forcing for one round |
| An immediate 3NT | Balanced, 18–19 HCP, stoppers in the other suits | Yes; proposes the final contract |

A stopper is a holding that prevents the opponents from immediately cashing all their tricks in a suit at notrump. It is not the same as length: we can have a stopper without four cards, and four cards without a secure stopper.

When several suits are available at the two level, show the cheapest stopper. After **1♣–2♣**, **2♦** shows diamond values. **2♥ or 2♠** shows values in that major. We are not searching for a 4–4 major fit, since responder has already denied a four-card major.

**2NT guarantees stoppers in both majors, not necessarily in the other minor**. Check that suit too before choosing 3NT.

### Responder's continuation

Responder knows whether the original raise was invitational or stronger. With the minimum of the 10+ range, responder may pass 2NT or three of the agreed minor. With game values, continue bidding.

Do not pass opener's new suit. Add information about our stoppers or choose notrump when the combined strength and coverage of the other suits are sufficient. In our simple version, **2NT or three of the agreed minor** may be passed unless a game force has already been established. Other continuations need appropriate strength and clear agreements.

### 1. Stopping in clubs

Our partnership auction is **1♣–2♣–3♣**. As responder, we hold:

♠ A84  
♥ K72  
♦ 85  
♣ QJ963

**Pass**. We have 10 HCP and opener has shown a minimum. Our initial 2♣ required one further bid, not a game contract.

### 2. Stopping in notrump

Our partnership auction is **1♦–2♦–2NT**. As responder, we hold:

♠ 84  
♥ K72  
♦ KJ963  
♣ K85

**Pass**. We have 10 HCP and opener has shown a balanced minimum. Our spades are not a problem because 2NT promises stoppers in both majors. In clubs we hold the king with two small cards.

### 3. Bidding game

Our partnership auction is **1♣–2♣–2♥**. As responder, we hold:

♠ AQ4  
♥ 83  
♦ KJ5  
♣ K9632

We bid **3NT**. We have 13 HCP, club support and stoppers in spades and diamonds. Opener showed a heart stopper. The 2♥ bid did not promise four hearts or ask for heart support.

## Points to remember

- **The raise to the two level is stronger** than the jump raise. Opener must not pass the initial 2♣ or 2♦.
- **Forcing for one round does not mean forcing to game.** Our version allows us to play 2NT or three of the agreed minor.
- **A four-card major comes first**, even with sufficient support for the minor.
- The new-suit bids discussed here show **stoppers or concentrated values**, without promising four cards.
- Before bidding **3NT**, consider the combined strength and stoppers, including the other minor.
- **6–9 points** for a jump raise is the choice in this lesson. Other versions use weaker hands. Agree which version your partnership plays.
- Use after an **overcall, double or an initial pass** requires separate agreements and is outside this lesson.
- Agree the strength ranges, minimum support length and continuations. Do not automatically import meanings from another version of Inverted Minors.
