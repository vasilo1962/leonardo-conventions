# Jacoby Transfer after 1NT

Partner opens 1NT and we hold at least a five-card major. With **Jacoby Transfer** we show our major and ask opener to bid it.

## When does it apply?

This lesson assumes a **balanced 1NT opening with 15–17 HCP**, without intervention by the opponents. We have at least five hearts or five spades.

A Transfer **has no minimum point requirement**: it can start a weak, invitational or strong sequence, even with zero HCP. Our next bid clarifies our strength.

We cover one long major and basic continuations. Both majors, strong two-suiters and slam exploration need further agreements and are outside this lesson.

## What does it achieve?

A Transfer shows our major while keeping the bidding low, leaving room to stop, invite game or bid game. When we play in that major, the 1NT opener declares: opener’s hand stays concealed and receives the opening lead.

The first Transfer shows **length, not a particular strength**. Opener waits for the continuation.

## Transfer bids and their acceptance

| Response to 1NT | Meaning | Opener’s bid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2♦** | At least five hearts, no minimum HCP | **2♥** |
| **2♥** | At least five spades, no minimum HCP | **2♠** |

2♦ and 2♥ are conventional: 2♦ asks for hearts and 2♥ asks for spades. They do not promise length in the suit named.

Without a five-card major we do not use Jacoby Transfer. With a four-card major and at least invitational values, we use Stayman if agreed. With game values, no four-card major and no slam interest, we may bid 3NT. Even long diamonds do not make 2♦ natural.

Opener **does not pass the Transfer**, but bids the requested major at the two level, even with only two cards. Acceptance defines neither strength nor fit.

## Examples of the initial Transfer

### Transfer with zero points

♠ 982  
♥ T98643  
♦ 73  
♣ 65

Partner opens 1NT. We bid **2♦** with six hearts and 0 HCP. After 2♥ we pass, aiming to play low in our long suit.

### The same first bid with game values

♠ AKJ83  
♥ 72  
♦ Q64  
♣ K85

Over 1NT we bid **2♥**, showing five spades and 13 HCP. After 2♠ we continue with 3NT, allowing opener to choose 3NT or 4♠.

## How do we continue after acceptance?

Responder’s next bid combines **strength and major length**. With five cards we leave opener a choice between the major and no trumps. With six or more, an eight-card fit is certain: the balanced 1NT opening promises at least two cards in every suit.

**1NT – 2♦ – 2♥**:

| Our next bid | What we show | What we ask of opener |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Weak, usually 0–7 HCP | Play 2♥ |
| **2NT** | About 8–9 HCP, exactly five hearts | Choose level and strain |
| **3♥** | Invitational, at least six hearts | Pass or bid 4♥ |
| **3NT** | Game values, exactly five hearts | Choose 3NT or 4♥ |
| **4♥** | Game values, at least six hearts | Play 4♥ |

There is also **Texas Transfer** for game-going hands with at least a six-card major. It will be covered in a separate lesson.

The same applies after **1NT – 2♥ – 2♠**: with five spades continue with 2NT or 3NT; with six or more invite with 3♠ or bid 4♠. Here 8–9 HCP is the usual invitation and about 10 HCP is enough for game, considering length and suit quality. These are evaluation guidelines; our examples are clear. Game bids assume no slam interest and no second suit to describe.

### Pass with a five-card major

♠ T84  
♥ Q9763  
♦ 85  
♣ J72

After **1NT – 2♦ – 2♥**, pass with 3 HCP and five hearts. We lack invitational strength and need not find three-card support to stop at the two level.

### Pass with a six-card major

♠ QJ9864  
♥ 73  
♦ 85  
♣ K92

After **1NT – 2♥ – 2♠**, pass with 6 HCP and six spades. Length guarantees a fit, but that alone does not justify bidding higher.

### Invite with a five-card major

♠ K83  
♥ QJ974  
♦ 86  
♣ Q52

After **1NT – 2♦ – 2♥**, bid **2NT** with 8 HCP and exactly five hearts. Opener chooses both level and strain.

### Invite with a six-card major

♠ AJ9864  
♥ K83  
♦ 72  
♣ 95

After **1NT – 2♥ – 2♠**, bid **3♠** with 8 HCP, six spades and a known fit. This is an invitation and may be passed.

### Choose game with a five-card major

♠ K92  
♥ AQT63  
♦ Q85  
♣ J4

After **1NT – 2♦ – 2♥**, bid **3NT** with 12 HCP, exactly five hearts and balanced shape. In this lesson’s basic agreement, opener passes with two hearts or chooses 4♥ with three or more.

### Game with a six-card major

♠ AKQ864  
♥ 92  
♦ K73  
♣ 85

After **1NT – 2♥ – 2♠**, bid **4♠** with 12 HCP and six spades headed by strong honours. We have at least an eight-card fit and enough for game.

### Opener’s next decision

Over an invitational 2NT, opener evaluates strength and fit. With a minimum and two cards in the major, pass; with a minimum and three or more, 3 of the major is to play. Accepting the invitation, choose 3NT with two cards or 4 of the major with three or more.

Over an invitation of 3 of the major, pass or bid 4 according to hand evaluation. Not every 16-HCP hand automatically accepts game.

## Points to remember

- **A Transfer promises no points.** Opener accepts because partner may want to stop low.
- **Accepting the Transfer shows neither a fit nor a minimum.** Opener bids the requested major [Superaccepting Jacoby Transfer](../jacoby-superaccept/).
- **2NT and 3 of the major are invitations.** Opener may pass or choose the appropriate contract.
- **3NT after a Transfer offers a choice of games.** With a five-card major we do not unilaterally insist on no trumps.
