# Michaels Cue Bid

## When does it apply?

Our right-hand opponent opens one of a suit. By bidding the same suit at the two level, we describe a two-suited hand. This is a conventional bid: it does not show length in the opponents' suit.

In the version presented here, we require at least **5 cards in each of the two suits**.

## What is its purpose?

The Michaels Cue Bid lets us show two long suits with one bid. Partner can choose which suit to play in, without our having to bid both suits separately.

Over a minor opening, we show both majors. Over a major opening, we show the other major and a minor, without yet specifying which minor.

## What does each bid mean?

| Opponent's opening | Our bid | Suits shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1♣ | 2♣ | At least 5 hearts and 5 spades |
| 1♦ | 2♦ | At least 5 hearts and 5 spades |
| 1♥ | 2♥ | At least 5 spades and a minor of at least 5 cards |
| 1♠ | 2♠ | At least 5 hearts and a minor of at least 5 cards |

We play Michaels with a **weak or very strong hand**. With intermediate strength, start with a natural overcall.

With a weak hand, we usually pass partner's simple preference. With a very strong hand, we continue bidding to show our extra strength.

Some partnerships use Michaels with any strength, provided they have the right distribution. In the version presented here, intermediate hands start with a natural overcall.

Michaels bids are conventional and partner must respond when the next opponent passes. The weak hand may pass partner's cheapest preference.

## Examples

### 1. Both majors

Our right-hand opponent opens 1♦ and we hold:

♠ KJ864  
♥ QJ975  
♦ 8  
♣ 62

We bid **2♦**, showing at least 5 spades and 5 hearts. We describe both suits with one bid. If partner chooses a major economically and the opponents pass, we also pass.

### 2. The other major and a minor

Our right-hand opponent opens 1♥ and we hold:

♠ AQ975  
♥ 6  
♦ 84  
♣ KJ963

We bid **2♥**, showing at least 5 spades and a minor of at least 5 cards. Partner knows about our spades but not which minor we hold. Partner may choose spades or ask with **2NT**. We answer **3♣** to the enquiry.

### 3. A very strong hand

Our right-hand opponent opens 1♣ and we hold:

♠ AKJ84  
♥ AQ975  
♦ 6  
♣ K2

We bid **2♣**, showing both majors. If partner chooses **2♥**, we continue with **3♥** to show the very strong hand. With a little help, partner will bid game.

## How do we continue?

The following responses apply when the next opponent passes.

### When Michaels shows both majors

Choose the major in which we have more cards. The cheapest preference does not promise points: we may have none at all.

With equal length in the majors, we usually choose hearts to stay lower.

### When Michaels shows a major and an unknown minor

We can choose the known major. If we prefer partner's minor, we bid **2NT**, asking partner to name it:

| Answer to 2NT | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 3♣ | The minor is clubs |
| 3♦ | The minor is diamonds |

**2NT is a conventional enquiry**, not a natural invitation to play in notrump. It does not by itself promise a strong hand. Before asking, we must be willing to play in either minor partner shows.

### Response example 1. Preference for a major

Our left-hand opponent opens 1♦, partner bids 2♦ Michaels and our right-hand opponent passes. We hold:

♠ J83  
♥ 9642  
♦ A7  
♣ 8654

We bid **2♥**. Partner has at least 5 cards in each major, so we choose hearts, where we have greater length.

### Response example 2. Finding the minor

Our left-hand opponent opens 1♥, partner bids 2♥ Michaels and our right-hand opponent passes. We hold:

♠ 7  
♥ Q862  
♦ J975  
♣ K864

We bid **2NT**, asking partner to name the minor. We have only one spade but 4-card support for both minors. If partner answers **3♣** or **3♦**, we pass.

## Points to remember

- At least **5 cards in each suit** are required. Some partnerships accept 5–4 in the majors over a minor opening; that is not the version we use here.
- We play Michaels with a **weak or very strong hand**. Intermediate hands start with a natural overcall. Some partnerships use it with any strength.
- Distribution alone is not enough. Consider suit quality and vulnerability, especially when partner's response takes us to the three level.
- Partner's cheapest preference **does not promise points or a guaranteed fit**. With the weak hand, do not continue simply to show the same distribution again.

The **Michaels Cue Bid is usually played together with [Unusual 2NT](../unusual-2nt/)**. The two conventions show different combinations of suits.
