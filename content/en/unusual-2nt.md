# Unusual 2NT

## When does it apply?

Our right-hand opponent opens one of a suit and we make a **jump overcall of 2NT**.

## What is its purpose?

Unusual 2NT shows at least **5–5 in the two lowest-ranking unbid suits**. We describe two suits at once and help partner choose where to play.

The bid is conventional: **it does not show a balanced hand or suggest playing in notrump**.

## What does each bid mean?

| Opponent's opening | Our bid | Suits shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1♣ | 2NT | At least 5 diamonds and 5 hearts |
| 1♦ | 2NT | At least 5 clubs and 5 hearts |
| 1♥ | 2NT | At least 5 clubs and 5 diamonds |
| 1♠ | 2NT | At least 5 clubs and 5 diamonds |

Unusual 2NT therefore **does not always show both minors**. It does so only over a major opening.

As with Michaels, our version uses it with a **weak or very strong hand**. With intermediate strength, start with a natural overcall. Some partnerships use it with any strength, provided they have the right distribution.

## Examples

### 1. After a 1♣ opening

Our right-hand opponent opens 1♣ and we hold:

♠ 6  
♥ KJ975  
♦ QJ864  
♣ 62

We bid **2NT**, showing at least 5 diamonds and 5 hearts: the two lowest-ranking unbid suits.

### 2. After a 1♦ opening

Our right-hand opponent opens 1♦ and we hold:

♠ 84  
♥ AQ975  
♦ 6  
♣ KJ963

We bid **2NT**, showing at least 5 clubs and 5 hearts. This time the opponent has bid diamonds, so the two lowest available suits are clubs and hearts.

### 3. After a major opening

Our right-hand opponent opens 1♠ and we hold:

♠ 6  
♥ 84  
♦ KJ975  
♣ QJ864

We bid **2NT**, showing at least 5 clubs and 5 diamonds. We would make the same bid over **1♥**: in both cases, we show both minors.

## How does partner respond?

When the next opponent passes, choose one of the two suits shown by 2NT. With a weak hand, bid at the lowest available level. A simple preference **does not promise points or a guaranteed fit**.

Usually prefer the suit in which we have more cards. With equal length, consider suit quality and the level of the auction.

### 1. Choosing between the minors

Our left-hand opponent opens 1♠, partner bids 2NT and our right-hand opponent passes. We hold:

♠ 842  
♥ K763  
♦ Q85  
♣ 972

We bid **3♦**. We have 3 cards in each minor, but the diamond queen may be more useful than the small clubs.

### 2. Choosing between hearts and diamonds

Our left-hand opponent opens 1♣, partner bids 2NT and our right-hand opponent passes. We hold:

♠ 8742  
♥ Q963  
♦ 85  
♣ K72

We bid **3♥**. The 2NT overcall shows diamonds and hearts, so we choose hearts, where we have 4-card support.

## Points to remember

- Unusual 2NT **does not always show both minors**. Its meaning depends on the opponent's opening.
- We require at least **5–5**. A 5–4 or 6–4 hand is not enough in our version.
- Consider suit quality and vulnerability: partner will usually have to bid at the three level.
- With the weak hand, usually pass partner's simple preference. With the very strong hand, continue to show the extra strength.

**Unusual 2NT is usually played together with the [Michaels Cue Bid](../michaels-cue-bid/)**. The two conventions show different combinations of suits.
