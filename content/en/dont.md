# DONT

## When does it apply?

DONT is used in both direct and balancing seat over the opponents' strong **1NT** opening.

## What is its purpose?

DONT lets us describe one-suited and two-suited hands, usually staying at the two level. The aim is to find a suitable contract while making the opponents' bidding more difficult.

The main difference from other defences to 1NT is that **double is not a penalty double: it shows a one-suited hand**.

## What does each bid mean?

| Our call after 1NT | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Double | A one-suited hand, without yet identifying the suit |
| 2♣ | Clubs and a higher-ranking suit: diamonds, hearts or spades |
| 2♦ | Diamonds and a major |
| 2♥ | Hearts and spades |
| 2♠ | A natural spade bid |

For two-suited hands, we require at least **5–4**; the lower-ranking suit does not have to be the five-card suit. For our one-suited examples, we use a **6-card or longer suit**.

DONT allows overcalls even with few high-card points, provided we have suitable distribution and adequate suit quality. Being able to stay at the two level encourages this aggressive approach, particularly in balancing seat.

Vulnerability also affects our evaluation.

## Examples

In all the following hands, our right-hand opponent has opened a strong 1NT.

### 1. A one-suited heart hand

♠ 83  
♥ KQJ974  
♦ A62  
♣ 75

We **double**. If partner responds 2♣ and the opponents pass, we bid **2♥**. An immediate 2♥ overcall would show both majors.

### 2. Clubs and spades

♠ KJ84  
♥ 6  
♦ 972  
♣ AQ965

We bid **2♣**, showing clubs and a higher-ranking suit. If partner asks for the second suit with 2♦, we bid **2♠**.

### 3. Diamonds and spades

♠ KJ84  
♥ 6  
♦ AQ975  
♣ 862

We bid **2♦**, showing diamonds and a major. If partner bids 2♥ to find our major, we correct to **2♠**.

### 4. Both majors

♠ KJ84  
♥ AQ975  
♦ 6  
♣ 862

We bid **2♥**, showing at least 5–4 in hearts and spades. Partner can pass or choose spades.

### 5. A one-suited spade hand

♠ QJ9864  
♥ 7  
♦ K82  
♣ 963

We bid **2♠**, naturally. In our version, a direct 2♠ overcall shows a weaker hand than double followed by 2♠.

## How does partner respond?

The following continuations assume that the opponents pass.

### After double

We usually bid **2♣**, allowing partner to show the suit:

| Continuation by the doubler | Suit |
| --- | --- |
| Pass | Clubs |
| 2♦ | Diamonds |
| 2♥ | Hearts |
| 2♠ | Spades |

The 2♣ response does not promise clubs. We do not leave the double in as though it were for penalties.

### After 2♣ or 2♦

We can pass if the known suit is suitable. Otherwise, bid the next suit as **pass or correct**: ask partner to pass if that is the second suit, or to name it.

After **2♣**, **2♦** asks for the second suit. After **2♦**, **2♥** asks for the major.

### After 2♥

Pass with a preference for hearts, or bid **2♠** with a preference for spades.

### Response example: staying in the known suit

Our left-hand opponent opens 1NT, partner bids 2♣ and our right-hand opponent passes. We hold:

♠ Q742  
♥ 83  
♦ K962  
♣ J75

We pass. We have 3-card club support. If we look for the second suit, it may be hearts, where we have only two cards.

### Response example: finding the major

In the same position, partner bids 2♦ and we hold:

♠ Q864  
♥ K975  
♦ 6  
♣ J842

We bid **2♥**, pass or correct. We have support for both majors: partner passes with hearts or bids 2♠ with spades.

## Points to remember

- **2♥ shows two suits**, not a one-suited heart hand.
- The second suit in a two-suited overcall always ranks higher than the first.
- Looking for the second suit does not guarantee a better fit.
- **2NT by partner** is an enquiry with game interest and requires agreed continuations. It is not the simple search for the second suit.
