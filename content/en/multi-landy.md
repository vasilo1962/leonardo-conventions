# Multi Landy

## When does it apply?

Multi Landy is a conventional defence to an opponent's natural 1NT opening.

Before using it, agree with partner whether you play it against a strong 1NT, a weak 1NT, or both.

In the version presented here, double is for penalties and 2♦ shows one long suit, which can be any of the four suits.

## What is its purpose?

It lets us describe hands with one long suit or two suits. This helps partner choose a suitable trump suit and makes it harder for the opponents to find their own contract.

Most of these overcalls are conventional: they do not necessarily show the suit we bid.

## What does each overcall mean?

| Overcall | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Double** | Penalty: we believe we can defeat 1NT. |
| **2♣** | Both majors, at least 5–4. |
| **2♦** | One suit with at least 6 cards, usually a major. |
| **2♥** | 5 hearts and a minor, usually 5 cards. A good 4-card minor is acceptable. |
| **2♠** | 5 spades and a minor, usually 5 cards. A good 4-card minor is acceptable. |
| **2NT** | Both minors, at least 5–5. |
| **3♣ / 3♦** | A natural overcall in that minor. |

The decision to overcall depends on distribution, suit quality, vulnerability and the strength of the opponents' 1NT.

A penalty double is not based solely on counting points. We need defensive tricks and a suitable opening lead.

## Examples of overcalls

In the following examples, our right-hand opponent opens 1NT. For the distributional overcalls, assume we are not vulnerable.

### 1. Both majors

♠ KQ96  
♥ AJ853  
♦ 7  
♣ Q62

We bid **2♣**. We have 5 hearts and 4 spades. We describe both majors with one bid and let partner choose.

### 2. One long suit

♠ AQJ964  
♥ 852  
♦ K72  
♣ 7

We bid **2♦**, showing a one-suited hand. We do not bid 2♠, because in Multi Landy that overcall also promises a minor.

### 3. Hearts and a minor

♠ 7  
♥ KQJ86  
♦ AQ974  
♣ 82

We bid **2♥**. We show 5 hearts and a minor. Partner knows about the hearts but does not yet know which minor we hold.

### 4. Spades and a minor

♠ KJ853  
♥ 6  
♦ 84  
♣ AQ974

We bid **2♠**. We show 5 spades and a minor, clubs in this case.

### 5. Both minors

♠ 7  
♥ 84  
♦ KQJ96  
♣ AJ874

We bid **2NT**. We show at least 5 clubs and 5 diamonds. This 2NT is conventional and does not show a balanced hand.

### 6. A penalty double

♠ AQJ  
♥ KQ4  
♦ KJ93  
♣ K82

Against a weak 1NT, for example 12–14 points, we **double**. We have a strong hand with defensive values. We want to defend 1NT doubled, not ask partner to choose a suit.

## How do we respond?

The following responses assume that the next opponent passes.

### After the 2♣ overcall

We try to find the best major-suit fit.

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **2♥ / 2♠** | Preference for that major, without inviting game. |
| **2♦** | Equal length in the majors. Ask partner to bid the longer major. |
| **3♥ / 3♠** | At least 4-card support and a game invitation. |

**An example of preference**

♠ A842  
♥ 97  
♦ J843  
♣ 975

We respond **2♠**. We have 4 spades and only 2 hearts. Partner has at least 4 spades, so we have at least 8 trumps together.

With the hand from the first overcall example, partner passes. Partner does not correct to the 5-card heart suit, since we have shown a preference for spades.

**An example with no preference**

♠ 852  
♥ Q96  
♦ A843  
♣ 974

We respond **2♦**. We have 3 cards in each major and leave the choice to partner.

With the hand from the first example, partner bids **2♥**. We find an 8-card heart fit rather than a 7-card spade fit.

### After the 2♦ overcall

We do not yet know partner's long suit. Our usual response is **2♥**, which is **pass or correct**: partner passes with hearts, otherwise bids the long suit.

The overcaller:

- Passes with hearts.
- Bids 2♠ with spades.
- Bids 3♣ with clubs.
- Bids 3♦ with diamonds.

**Example**

With the second overcall hand, the auction develops as follows:

| West | North | East | South |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1NT | 2♦ | Pass | 2♥ |
| Pass | **2♠** | | |

South's 2♥ did not show hearts. It allowed North to reveal the long suit.

A **2♠** response is also available when we are willing to play at least 3♥ if partner has hearts. It is not a natural bid showing spades. Strong responses, such as the 2NT enquiry, require additional agreements about the continuations.

### After a 2♥ or 2♠ overcall

We know partner's major but not the minor.

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | We choose to play in the major. |
| **2NT** | Ask partner to bid the minor: 3♣ or 3♦. |
| **Another suit** | Natural, usually at least 6 good cards, to play there. |
| **3 of partner's major** | In our version, a game invitation. |

**Example**

Partner overcalls **2♥** with the third overcall hand. We do not want to play in hearts, so we ask for the minor with **2NT**. Partner bids **3♦**.

Here, 2NT does not suggest playing in notrump.

### After the 2NT overcall

We know partner has at least 5–5 in the minors.

With a weak hand, choose **3♣ or 3♦** according to our support. We do not pass the conventional 2NT simply because we have few points.

With a strong hand, consider game, taking account of the fit, tricks and stoppers.

### After the penalty double

We usually **pass** and defend 1NT doubled.

With a very weak hand and a suit of our own with at least 5 cards, we may make a natural escape bid. We do not automatically run to a 4-card suit merely because we have very few points.

## Points to remember

- **2♣** shows the majors.
- **2♦** shows one long suit.
- **2♥ and 2♠** overcalls also promise a minor.
- **2NT** as an overcall shows the minors, but as a response to 2♥ or 2♠ asks for the minor.
- Versions of Multi Landy differ. In particular, the meaning of double, whether 2♦ includes a minor, and invitational or preemptive raises must be agreed by the partnership.
