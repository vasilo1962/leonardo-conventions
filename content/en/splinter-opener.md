# Splinter by opener

**A Splinter is a double jump in a new suit.** Opener uses it after partner's response to show at least four-card support for the suit of the response, a singleton or void in the suit bid, and game-going strength. In **1♣ – 1♥ – 4♦**, 4♦ is a Splinter: it shows heart support and a singleton or void in diamonds. It is **game forcing**. [Splinter by responder](../splinter-responder/) is a separate lesson.

## When does it apply?

We open **1♣ or 1♦** and partner responds **1♥ or 1♠**, promising at least four cards. We hold **at least four-card support**, a singleton or void elsewhere and enough strength for game.

Our version covers a natural minor-suit opening in first or second seat, an unpassed responder and opponents who pass throughout. Applications after a two-level response or later in the auction need separate agreement.

## What does it achieve?

In bridge, the location of honors affects the combined strength. Opener immediately shows **support and a singleton or void**, allowing responder to revalue the hand.

Partner revalues the hand. Low cards opposite our singleton or void are favorable: partner's strength lies elsewhere. Kings, queens and jacks in that suit may be worth less than their point count suggests. An ace remains useful opposite a singleton; opposite a void its value depends on the whole hand.

The Splinter shows support for responder's major. **It commits the partnership to game, not slam.**

## What does each bid show?

| Opening | Response | Opener's Splinter | Support | Singleton or void |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1♣ | 1♥ | 3♠ | ≥4 ♥ | ♠ |
| 1♣ | 1♥ | 4♦ | ≥4 ♥ | ♦ |
| 1♦ | 1♥ | 3♠ | ≥4 ♥ | ♠ |
| 1♦ | 1♥ | 4♣ | ≥4 ♥ | ♣ |
| 1♣ | 1♠ | 4♦ | ≥4 ♠ | ♦ |
| 1♣ | 1♠ | 4♥ | ≥4 ♠ | ♥ |
| 1♦ | 1♠ | 4♣ | ≥4 ♠ | ♣ |
| 1♦ | 1♠ | 4♥ | ≥4 ♠ | ♥ |

All these rebids are **artificial and game forcing**.

### How much strength?

**To make a Splinter, we need game values opposite even the minimum partner has promised, considering high-card points, support and distribution together. This applies to both opener and responder.**

A 1♥ or 1♠ response shows at least 6 HCP. Opener therefore needs enough extra strength and distribution to commit to game instead of making a simple or invitational raise.

The Splinter describes a relatively limited hand. Slam may emerge when the hands fit well; a much stronger hand needs another route according to the partnership's system. Exact limits are agreed: no single range applies to every version. Our examples use 18 HCP with clear distribution; 18 is neither a compulsory nor an exclusive limit.

## Examples

The opponents pass throughout. Choose opener's rebid after partner's response.

### 1. 1♣ – 1♥ – 4♦

♠ KJ3  
♥ AK74  
♦ 6  
♣ AQJ85

Bid **4♦ Splinter**: 18 HCP, four-card heart support, a good five-card club suit and a singleton diamond. Show game values and let partner assess the honors knowing where our singleton is.

### 2. 1♦ – 1♥ – 3♠

♠ —  
♥ AQ74  
♦ AKJ85  
♣ KJ83

Bid **3♠ Splinter**: 18 HCP, four-card heart support and a spade void. The same bid could show a singleton spade. It shows heart support and the partnership cannot stop below game.

### 3. 1♦ – 1♠ – 4♣

♠ AK74  
♥ KJ3  
♦ AQJ85  
♣ 6

Bid **4♣ Splinter**: 18 HCP, four-card spade support and a singleton club. Knowing about the support and singleton helps responder judge whether game is enough or slam is worth exploring.

### 4. 1♣ – 1♥ – 2♥

♠ K83  
♥ KJ74  
♦ 6  
♣ AJ852

Bid **2♥**, a simple raise: 12 HCP, four-card support and a singleton diamond, but insufficient strength to force game opposite a minimum response. A 4♦ Splinter would describe a much stronger hand.

## How do we continue?

After opener's Splinter, **responder revalues the hand**: trump quality, useful honors outside the Splinter suit and controls matter, not just the total points.

| Responder's assessment | Continuation |
| --- | --- |
| No slam prospects. | Bid four of the agreed major; opener passes. |
| A suitable hand with slam interest. | Make an available control bid (cue bid) below game, or ask for aces. |

We use **first- or second-round controls**: an ace or void, a king or singleton. When showing a control, bid the cheapest available new suit containing one. We are not looking for a different fit.

Check the danger of two quick losers elsewhere. If the Splinter leaves room only for game in the major, do not invent a control bid below it; further exploration requires appropriate partnership agreements.

### 1. 1♣ – 1♥ – 4♦ – 4♥

♠ Q83  
♥ KJ74  
♦ KQ4  
♣ T62

Bid **4♥**, to play: 11 HCP, but five are in diamonds opposite a singleton or void. The remaining cards do not justify slam exploration. Opener has described strength and distribution and passes.

### 2. 1♣ – 1♥ – 3♠ – 4♣

♠ 864  
♥ KQ74  
♦ AQ3  
♣ A85

Bid **4♣**, showing a club control and slam interest: 15 HCP, all outside spades, good trumps and useful minor-suit honors. Show the cheapest control first without deciding the final contract.

### 3. 1♦ – 1♠ – 4♣ – 4♦

♠ AQ74  
♥ AK3  
♦ K85  
♣ 862

Bid **4♦**: 16 HCP, no club points and good trumps. The diamond king is a second-round control. Show it before a heart control instead of skipping diamonds to bid 4♥.

After a control bid, opener cooperates according to partnership agreements. Ask for aces or key cards once controls and trump quality are sufficiently known. **Do not automatically bid 4NT after every Splinter.** With a void, simply counting aces may not tell us which are useful.

If responder bids game without showing slam interest, opener **passes**, even with a maximum within the agreed range. Do not repeat the invitation after describing the hand.

## Points to remember

- **At least four-card support is required.** The major response promises at least four cards, not necessarily five.
- **Game values opposite partner's minimum are required.** Support and a singleton or void alone are insufficient.
- **Splinter at the three level.** After 1♥, 3♠ shows a singleton or void in spades: the only three-level Splinter in the sequences covered here.

- **Avoid a singleton ace or king in our version.** Partner could misjudge the other honors in that suit.
- **Not every jump is a Splinter.** In 1♣ – 1♥ – 3♦ there is only one jump, not the double jump of 4♦. Natural bids and rebids of opener's suit have their own meanings.
